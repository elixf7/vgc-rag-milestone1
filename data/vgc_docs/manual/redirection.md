# Redirection: Follow Me and Rage Powder

Redirection moves (Follow Me, Rage Powder) pull all single-target attacks from the
opposing side onto the redirector for that turn. They are the backbone of setup
and support strategies in doubles: a redirector buys a free turn for its partner
to set up Trick Room, Tailwind, a stat-boosting move, or a guaranteed KO.

Rage Powder fails against Grass-types and Pokémon with Overcoat or holding a
Safety Goggles, because it is a powder move. Follow Me has no such restriction but
can still be ignored by moves that target all opponents (spread moves) and by
certain abilities. Snatch and Taunt do not stop redirection, but a faster Fake Out
flinch does.

To beat redirection: (1) use spread moves that hit both opponents and ignore the
redirect, (2) Fake Out or otherwise disable the redirector before it acts, (3)
target the redirector itself for a KO, or (4) use moves with the ability to bypass
redirection. Amoonguss-style Rage Powder users are especially common; bring a
Grass-type, Safety Goggles holder, or spread attacker to play around them.

When you hold the redirector, protect your win condition: redirect the turn your
partner is most vulnerable, and remember the opponent may Fake Out your redirector
to break the strategy.
