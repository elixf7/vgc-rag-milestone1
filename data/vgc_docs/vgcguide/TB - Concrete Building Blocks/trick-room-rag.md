## Trick Room — Mechanic Overview

**Trick Room** reverses the Speed order of all Pokémon on the field for five turns. Under its effect, the Pokémon with the lowest Speed stat moves first, and the Pokémon with the highest Speed stat moves last. **Trick Room** does not affect move priority — priority brackets still resolve normally. **Trick Room** itself has -7 priority, making it almost always the last move resolved in the turn it is used. Because it resolves last on the turn it is set, its practical effect lasts four turns rather than the full five.

## Trick Room — Why It Is Strategically Strong

**Trick Room** offers several structural advantages over other speed control strategies:

- Many of the strongest offensive and defensive Pokémon have low Speed as a counterbalance to their high attacking and bulk stats. **Trick Room** converts that low Speed from a liability into a first-mover advantage.
- Pokémon built for **Trick Room** typically invest no EVs in Speed, freeing those points for HP, Defense, Special Defense, or offensive stats. This gives them a meaningful stat advantage over Pokémon that must invest in Speed to remain competitive outside **Trick Room**.
- In doubles, **Trick Room** is substantially more impactful than in singles because two Pokémon per side can take advantage of each turn under the effect, rather than one.
- **Trick Room** has been a dominant or top-tier strategy across every VGC format historically, making it one of the most consistently viable team archetypes in the game.

## Trick Room — Team Structure

A standard dedicated **Trick Room** team includes the following roles:

- **Setters (2)**: Pokémon that know and use **Trick Room**.
- **Helpers (1–2)**: Pokémon whose primary goal is to protect the setter and ensure **Trick Room** goes up.
- **Sweepers (1–2)**: Pokémon that deal the bulk of the damage while **Trick Room** is active.

This structure — often called a "hard Trick Room team" — is strongest when **Trick Room** is successfully set and weaker if it cannot be established. Many dedicated **Trick Room** teams address this by including an alternate mode or at least one fast Pokémon to function outside of **Trick Room** if needed.

Teams can also use **Trick Room** more lightly: a team with only one or two slow Pokémon can still include **Trick Room** as a disruptive option or situational mode without committing the entire structure to it.

## Trick Room — Setter Role

Every **Trick Room** team needs at least one Pokémon that knows **Trick Room** — a "setter." Setters typically fall into two categories:

**Bulky setters**: Trade offense for survivability. Their primary goal is to get **Trick Room** up and then provide ongoing support. Examples include **Cresselia**, **Dusclops**, and **Porygon2**. These Pokémon are naturally difficult to KO in one hit, reducing the need for extensive setup support.

**Offensive setters**: Need more support to get **Trick Room** up safely, but immediately become offensive threats once it is active — no repositioning needed. Examples include **Hatterene** and **Reuniclus**. Offensive setters pair best with defensive helpers (redirection users) that absorb the pressure on the setup turn.

Some setters are exceptions to both categories. Fast or frail Pokémon (**Nihilego**, **Whimsicott**, **Gengar**) can be used as setters for surprise value or as anti-speed-control tech, exploiting the assumption that those Pokémon would not run **Trick Room**.

## Trick Room — Helper Role

**Trick Room** helpers protect the setter on the turn **Trick Room** is being used, ensuring it resolves before the setter is KO'd or disrupted. Helpers typically have low Speed themselves (to function under **Trick Room** after it goes up) and access to strong support moves. The two primary support methods are:

**Fake Out**: Flinches one opposing Pokémon, preventing it from acting that turn. This means the setter only needs to survive one incoming attack rather than two in order to set **Trick Room**. **Fake Out** users with **Intimidate** (e.g., **Incineroar**, **Scrafty**) provide additional defensive value by weakening opposing physical attackers on the same turn.

**Redirection (Follow Me / Rage Powder)**: Draws both opponents' single-target attacks toward the redirector, shielding the setter completely for that turn. Redirection users tend to be defensively oriented. A notable outcome: if the redirector is KO'd on the setup turn, **Trick Room** goes up and a free switch can be made to a more offensive Pokémon. Each redirector also offers its own disruptive tools — **Amoonguss** can follow up with **Spore**; **Indeedee-F** can follow up with **Expanding Force** under its own **Psychic Terrain**.

Defensive redirection helpers pair best with offensive setters. Offensive helpers (**Fake Out** + **Intimidate**) are more flexible and work well with setters of either type.

## Trick Room — Sweeper Role

**Trick Room** sweepers are the primary damage dealers while **Trick Room** is active. They are typically very slow, very bulky, and hit extremely hard. The goal is to deal maximum damage across the four effective **Trick Room** turns before the effect expires.

Key traits:
- Broad move coverage and the ability to threaten many Pokémon types make sweepers harder to safely switch into.
- High natural bulk ensures they survive long enough to attack repeatedly.
- Very low Speed is an asset under **Trick Room** but a major liability outside of it — sweepers caught when **Trick Room** is down will consistently move last.
- Common items: **Life Orb** (maximizes damage output), **Weakness Policy** (powerful if triggered on a hit the sweeper can survive).

Examples of **Trick Room** sweepers: **Rhyperior**, **Torkoal**, **Glastrier**.

## Trick Room — Counters and Counterplay

Several tools can prevent or disrupt **Trick Room** from being set:

- **Taunt**: Prevents the setter from using **Trick Room** for three turns. Note that **Mental Herb** allows a Pokémon to cure **Taunt** once, so dedicated setters may carry it.
- **Phazing moves (Roar, Dragon Tail, Circle Throw)**: These moves have negative priority and resolve before **Trick Room** (which has -7 priority), forcing the setter to switch out before **Trick Room** activates.
- **Re-using Trick Room**: If **Trick Room** is already active, using **Trick Room** again cancels it immediately, returning to normal Speed order.
- **Imprison + Trick Room**: A Pokémon that knows both **Imprison** and **Trick Room** can use **Imprison** to prevent all opposing Pokémon from using **Trick Room** as long as the **Imprison** user stays on the field.
- **Running slow Pokémon yourself**: A team that includes naturally slow Pokémon can take advantage of an opponent's **Trick Room** rather than being hurt by it, removing much of its strategic value.

Important caveat: dedicated **Trick Room** teams are typically built with counterplay to all of the above in the form of support Pokémon. **Fake Out** and redirection can neutralize **Taunt** attempts and phazing. The most consistently effective anti-**Trick Room** tools are **Taunt** (broadly flexible and useful in many matchups beyond just **Trick Room**) and building slow Pokémon into your own team (useful in many situations regardless of whether the opponent uses **Trick Room**).

## Trick Room — Integration Spectrum

**Trick Room** can be integrated into a team at many levels of commitment:

- **Hard Trick Room**: Full team built around the strategy — two setters, dedicated helpers, multiple low-Speed sweepers. Strongest when **Trick Room** goes up; most vulnerable if disrupted.
- **Trick Room mode**: One setter and one or two slow Pokémon that benefit, alongside a functional fast core. Provides the option without full dependency.
- **Trick Room as disruption**: Even a single slow Pokémon paired with a **Trick Room** setter can create significant pressure on speed-reliant opponents, functioning as a pivot strategy rather than a primary win condition.

There are no rigid rules about how many Pokémon must benefit from **Trick Room** for it to be worth including. A team with mostly fast Pokémon can still gain meaningful value from **Trick Room** as a disruption tool against other speed control strategies, particularly **Tailwind** teams.
