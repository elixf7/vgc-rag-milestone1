## Items — Role in VGC Teambuilding

Held items are equipped to individual Pokémon before a battle and take effect automatically during the match. Trainers cannot use consumable items (Potions, Revives, etc.) during a competitive battle — only held items matter. VGC enforces an item clause: each Pokémon on a team must hold a different item, so a six-Pokémon team will always carry six distinct held items. Item selection is one of the most consequential teambuilding decisions because the right item can significantly amplify a Pokémon's role, while the wrong item wastes a slot.

## Items — Evaluating Item Quality

When assessing whether an item is worth running, three factors determine its value:

**Activation frequency**: Items that are always active (e.g., **Choice Specs**, **Leftovers**) provide consistent value. Conditional items (e.g., **Sitrus Berry**, **Weakness Policy**) require specific thresholds or triggers. Items that rarely come into play across a full match are generally poor choices regardless of their effect when active.

**Benefit magnitude**: The impact of the effect must be meaningful relative to the situation. A small offensive boost (e.g., **Absorb Bulb** at +1) is far less impactful than a large one (e.g., **Weakness Policy** at +2). How strong an item is also depends heavily on the Pokémon holding it — **Focus Sash** is excellent on a fast, frail Pokémon that would otherwise be one-shot, but nearly useless on a naturally tanky Pokémon that is rarely KO'd in one hit.

**Opportunity cost**: The best item for a Pokémon is the one that outperforms all alternatives in that slot. An item like **Air Balloon** (grants Ground immunity until hit) is inconsistent because it can be circumvented, and most Pokémon that would consider it perform better with **Focus Sash** or a resist berry instead.

Most held items fall into four functional categories: recovery, damage output, damage mitigation, and support.

## Items — Recovery Category

Recovery items restore HP during battle, extending a Pokémon's staying power without requiring it to spend turns using recovery moves.

- **Sitrus Berry**: Restores a fixed amount of HP when the holder drops below half. Best on Pokémon with moderate to significant bulk — those that typically take two to four hits to KO — where the one-time heal has a meaningful impact.
- **Leftovers**: Restores a small amount of HP each turn. Most effective on Pokémon that stay on the field for many turns (at least four, to outpace the equivalent **Sitrus Berry** value) and on Pokémon with other passive recovery (**Leech Seed**, **Grassy Terrain**), since **Protect** allows them to stack the passive healing.
- **33% Berries (Figy, Mago, Aguav, Wiki, Iapapa)**: Restore 33% HP when the holder drops below a quarter of their maximum HP. Better than **Sitrus Berry** on Pokémon with enough bulk to consistently reach the activation threshold. Also useful on Pokémon with recoil moves, which can trigger the berry on demand.

## Items — Damage Output Category

Damage output items increase a Pokémon's offensive threat level. The strongest options carry drawbacks; the weaker options offer fewer strings attached.

- **Choice Band / Choice Specs**: Multiply the holder's Attack or Special Attack by 1.5×, but lock them into the first move selected after entering the field. Best on Pokémon with a clearly dominant move that needs no switching flexibility — a Pokémon that primarily deals damage and does not need **Protect** or other defensive options.
- **Life Orb**: Boosts all damage by 30% at the cost of HP recoil on every hit. Stronger than type-boosting items across a mixed moveset, but the recoil accumulates and reduces longevity.
- **Type-boosting items (Mystic Water, Miracle Seed, Magnet, etc.)**: Boost moves of a specific type by 20% with no drawback. Best on Pokémon that deal most of their damage through one type, value the ability to switch moves or use **Protect**, and where the gap between this and **Choice** items is less meaningful.
- **Weakness Policy**: Grants +2 Attack and +2 Special Attack when the holder is hit by a super effective move. Extremely powerful if triggered on a Pokémon that can survive the hit and immediately threaten the opponent, but unreliable on Pokémon too frail to survive the triggering hit.

Choosing between these items often comes down to whether the Pokémon needs move flexibility. A Pokémon locked into one primary attack with no need for **Protect** is a natural **Choice** item candidate. A Pokémon that rotates between multiple moves or relies on **Protect** for defensive positioning benefits more from **Life Orb** or a type-boosting item.

## Items — Damage Mitigation and Survivability Category

Survivability items do not restore HP but reduce incoming damage or prevent a KO, allowing a Pokémon to remain on the field longer.

- **Assault Vest**: Boosts the holder's Special Defense by 50% but prevents the use of Status-category moves, including **Protect**. Best on Pokémon that don't rely on **Protect** and want to patch up Special Defense to handle threats they would otherwise struggle to survive.
- **Focus Sash**: Preserves the holder at 1 HP if they would be KO'd from full HP in a single hit. Excellent on fast, frail Pokémon that need one additional turn on the field and that can maintain full HP through careful positioning or because they typically act before opponents. Loses all value if the holder takes any prior chip damage.
- **Eviolite**: Raises the Defense and Special Defense of a Pokémon that can still evolve by 50%. Extremely strong on deliberately un-evolved Pokémon (e.g., **Dusclops**, **Chansey**) built around exploiting this bulk.
- **Type resist berries (Shuca Berry, Coba Berry, Occa Berry, etc.)**: Halve the damage from one super effective hit of the corresponding type. Best used to patch a specific type weakness that would otherwise result in a clean KO, giving the holder one additional turn against that type of threat.

## Items — Support Category

Support items serve niche purposes that don't fit cleanly into recovery, offense, or defense.

- **Safety Goggles**: Grants immunity to powder moves (**Spore**, **Sleep Powder**) and weather damage. Best on Pokémon that would otherwise be shut down by sleep or that need to operate freely through opposing **Amoonguss** or similar powder users.
- **Lum Berry**: Cures any status condition once. Particularly valuable on Pokémon vulnerable to sleep or paralysis that need to remain active for multiple turns.
- **Light Clay**: Extends the duration of **Reflect**, **Light Screen**, and **Aurora Veil** from five turns to eight. Near-mandatory on dedicated screen setters, but commits the item slot, reducing the setter's flexibility.
- **Choice Scarf**: Multiplies the holder's Speed by 1.5×, locking them into the first move selected. Best on Pokémon that are threatening but need a Speed boost to outpace key threats — effectively the damage-output version of a Speed control item.

## Items — Choosing an Item: Bolster Strengths or Mitigate Weaknesses

Every strong item choice does one of two things for a Pokémon: it amplifies something it already does well, or it patches a gap that limits its effectiveness.

**Bolstering a strength:**
- High damage output with no move flexibility needed → **Choice Band**, **Choice Specs**, or **Life Orb**
- High damage output paired with good bulk or multi-type moveset → type-boosting item (e.g., **Magnet**, **Mystic Water**) or **Sitrus Berry**
- Defensive wall that stays on the field for many turns → **Leftovers**
- Threatening primarily because of Speed → **Choice Scarf**

**Mitigating a weakness:**
- Weak to a common type in the format → type resist berry (e.g., **Shuca Berry**, **Coba Berry**)
- Needs extra Special Defense to survive key hits → **Assault Vest**
- Fast and frail; often KO'd in one hit → **Focus Sash**
- Often KO'd in two hits → **Sitrus Berry**
- Frequently shut down by powder moves → **Safety Goggles**

The same item can serve either function depending on the Pokémon. **Sitrus Berry** on a naturally bulky Pokémon bolsters its strength (more staying power); **Sitrus Berry** on a Pokémon that barely survives two hits mitigates a weakness (pushes it from a two-hit KO to a three-hit KO).

## Items — Format Context and Item Selection

Which items are strongest shifts with the format. Formats where super effective coverage is common make resist berries and **Focus Sash** stronger. Formats with widespread physical or special attackers shift the value of **Assault Vest** or type resist berries. Formats where sleep is prevalent increase the value of **Safety Goggles** and **Lum Berry**. When building a team, identifying the most common threats in the format and matching item choices to those threats — while still amplifying each Pokémon's core role — is the key to effective itemization.
