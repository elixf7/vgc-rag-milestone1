## EV Spreads — Purpose and Core Concept

EV spreads are the primary tool for customizing a Pokémon's in-battle performance. Every Pokémon on a team was added for a specific role, and EVs should reinforce that role. Before building a spread, identify why the Pokémon is on the team — the spread should make that purpose more reliable.

EVs affect stats in two ways: **Speed** EVs determine when a Pokémon acts within a turn; all other EVs alter how the damage formula resolves offensive and defensive interactions.

## EV Spreads — Speed as the First Decision

**Speed** is the single most important stat to settle before allocating any other EVs. A one-point Speed advantage causes a Pokémon to act first, which can determine the outcome of a game. Decide the target Speed stat first — this sets the remaining EV budget for other stats.

Key considerations when choosing a Speed target:
- If a common, threatening Pokémon around your Pokémon's Speed can knock it out, and your Pokémon can knock back, prioritizing an outspeed investment is usually correct.
- In **Trick Room**, minimize Speed to move first under the reversed priority. Consider a Speed-dropping nature (e.g. **Brave** or **Quiet**) to push Speed lower.

## EV Spreads — Choosing Offensive vs. Defensive Investment

After Speed, decide whether remaining EVs emphasize offense or shore up a defensive gap. Two strategic approaches:

- **Lean into strengths**: Maximize the attacking stat (Attack or Special Attack) to increase damage output consistently. Best for fast, frail, or role-specific attackers.
- **Shore up weaknesses**: Invest in bulk to let a Pokémon survive hits it otherwise wouldn't. Note that a stat point in a lower base stat yields a greater proportional return in the damage formula than the same point in a high base stat — investing in a low defensive stat can be more efficient.

Either approach can be correct; the choice should follow the Pokémon's role on the team.

## EV Spreads — The 252/252/4 "Max-Max" Starting Spread

The simplest EV spread is 252 in two stats and 4 in a third (a "max-max" spread). This is a strong default for Pokémon whose role maps cleanly onto two stats. Always invest in 3 or 5 stats total — investing in 2, 4, or 6 stats wastes EVs that could be productive elsewhere.

**Example — fast physical attacker (e.g. Kartana):**
- EVs: 252 Atk / 4 SpD / 252 Spe
- Nature: **Jolly** (boosts Speed, drops Special Attack)
- Logic: maximizes physical damage and Speed; the 4 EVs go to a defensive stat rather than the unused Special Attack.

**Example — bulky special attacker (e.g. Sylveon):**
- EVs: 252 HP / 4 Def / 252 SpA
- Nature: **Modest** (boosts Special Attack, drops Attack)
- Logic: maximizes damage output while adding bulk through HP; the lower defensive stat receives the leftover 4 EVs.

## EV Spreads — Natures

A nature raises one stat by 10% and lowers another by 10%. When choosing a nature:

- Boost the stat most important to the Pokémon's role: **Speed** (**Jolly** / **Timid**), **Attack** (**Adamant**), **Special Attack** (**Modest**), or a defensive stat (**Bold** / **Calm** / **Impish** / **Careful**, etc.).
- Drop the attacking stat the Pokémon doesn't use. Physical attackers drop Special Attack; special attackers drop Attack. Utility moves that deal incidental damage (e.g. **Fake Out**) do not count.
- In **Trick Room**, dropping Speed via **Brave** or **Quiet** is often correct to move first under reversed priority.
- Dropping a defensive stat is a last resort — bulk is generally more valuable than a small offensive boost from a neutral nature.

Common natures by category:
- Offensive speed: **Jolly**, **Timid**
- Offensive damage: **Adamant**, **Modest**
- Trick Room offensive: **Brave**, **Quiet**
- Defensive: **Bold**, **Relaxed**, **Impish**, **Calm**, **Careful**, **Sassy**

## EV Spreads — Role-Based Spread Guidelines

Use the Pokémon's role to pick a starting framework:

**Fast and frail (or holding Focus Sash; base Speed ~96+, low bulk):**
- Maximize Speed and the relevant attacking stat.
- Use a nature that boosts Speed and drops the unused attacking stat.

**Mid-speed (base Speed ~60–95):**
- Check speed tiers to find a meaningful target — outrunning or underrunning a threatening Pokémon that your Pokémon can trade with is a common benchmark.
- If no clear speed target exists, 252 Speed or 252 HP are both reasonable defaults.
- Allocate remaining EVs to the attacking stat if damage output is the priority, or defensive stats if survivability matters more.

**Trick Room attacker:**
- Maximize HP and the relevant attacking stat.
- If HP is much higher than both defensive stats, redirect some EVs into the lower defensive stat.
- Keep Speed as low as possible; use a Speed-dropping nature.

**Dedicated support / utility (e.g. Tailwind setter, Trick Room setter, screen user):**
- Invest primarily in HP and one or both defensive stats.
- Choose Defense or Special Defense based on the threats the Pokémon needs to survive; split evenly if uncertain.
- Avoid running too many support Pokémon on one team — offensive power is necessary to close games.

**Eviolite holder:**
- Maximize HP to amplify the Eviolite's defensive multiplier.
- Split remaining EVs between Defense and Special Defense, or bias toward the lower stat for greater formula efficiency.

## EV Spreads — Standard Notation

EV spreads follow the order: HP / Attack / Defense / Special Attack / Special Defense / Speed.

- **Expanded**: 4 HP / 252 Atk / 252 Spe
- **Shorthand**: 4/252/0/0/0/252
- **Japanese**: 4H-252A-252S
