## Battling — Predictions: Two Distinct Skills

"Predicting" in VGC covers two separate skills that are easy to conflate:

1. **Anticipating the full range of possible plays**: mapping every combination of moves the opponent could make this turn. This supports finding the objectively best play — the one that performs well across the widest range of opponent responses.
2. **Calling a specific play**: committing to a read that the opponent will make one particular move and responding to that move specifically. This can create large swings but carries the risk of being wrong.

Both skills are worth developing. The first is the foundation of consistent decision-making; the second is a tool for creating advantages or recovering from bad positions.

## Battling — When to Go for a Specific Prediction

The decision to commit to a specific read depends on the current game state. Three scenarios:

**When ahead:**
- Go for a specific prediction when getting it right closes out the game, but getting it wrong does not immediately surrender the lead. The asymmetry in outcomes makes the risk acceptable.

**When neutral:**
- A specific prediction is worth attempting when getting it right produces a significant swing in your favor — enough to shift the momentum of the game meaningfully.

**When behind:**
- A specific prediction becomes necessary when there is no "safe" play that covers multiple opponent options. Common causes: a bad matchup from team selection, or falling behind too quickly to recover through neutral play.
- Look for situations where the opponent might make a mistake, since winning through correct play on both sides may no longer be possible.

## Battling — Knowing the Metagame as the Foundation for Predictions

Predicting well requires accurate prior knowledge of what opponents are likely to do. The relevant information to build up:

- Which Pokémon are commonly used in the current format.
- The most common items on each Pokémon (typically a short list of 3–4 options).
- The most common moves on each Pokémon (roughly the top 8–10).
- How that Pokémon is typically built regarding Speed — whether it is built to outpace specific benchmarks or run slow for a **Trick Room** team.
- Common in-game behaviors: what players typically do with that Pokémon in specific board states.

Without this baseline, accurate predictions are guesswork. Building metagame knowledge transforms prediction from speculation into informed probability assessment.

## Battling — Limits of Prediction-Focused Play

Predictions carry inherent risk and should not be the primary path to winning games. Key considerations:

- **Best-of-1 formats amplify surprise**: unexpected Pokémon, moves, items, and EV spreads can be decisive precisely because they cannot be predicted from prior knowledge. Leaning heavily on predictions in best-of-1 is especially dangerous.
- **Team quality reduces the need to predict**: strong teams create situations where a safe play exists on most turns. A team that forces constant high-stakes predictions has structural problems — the team is doing extra work that better construction would avoid.
- **Players have different styles**: some players are read-focused and play aggressively around specific predictions; others aim to minimize prediction altogether and play for safety. Neither approach is universally correct — the right balance depends on the player, the team, and the game state.
