## Protect — Role and Core Value in Doubles

**Protect** is one of the most fundamental moves in VGC. In Singles, **Protect** stalls one turn but changes nothing — the same threat remains next turn. In Doubles, the dynamic is entirely different: with a partner on the field, protecting one Pokémon while the partner attacks removes the threat entirely, rather than merely delaying it.

The core function of **Protect** is positional control. It keeps options open, creates uncertainty for the opponent, and prevents "wasted" turns where the opponent would otherwise have an uncontested advantage.

## Protect — Four Strategic Uses

**1. Threatening a trade the opponent doesn't want.**
When a Pokémon is targeted, using **Protect** forces the opponent to accept a disadvantageous outcome: attack into **Protect** and accomplish nothing, or redirect and risk the protected Pokémon acting freely next turn. This mind game applies constant pressure without requiring the Pokémon to attack.

**2. Positioning and board navigation.**
If two Pokémon are intended to be on the field together but only one is currently out, **Protect** allows a free switch — using the turn to bring in the correct partner without taking damage. This lets a player navigate toward favorable board states that would otherwise require taking a hit to achieve.

**3. Shielding an attacker while support arrives.**
**Protect** pairs naturally with supportive moves like **Fake Out**, **Intimidate**, **Follow Me** / **Rage Powder**, and **Tailwind**. Protecting a high-damage attacker for one turn while support is applied keeps it alive to deal more damage over the course of the game.

**4. Wasting turns of timed effects.**
Many effects in competitive Pokémon last a fixed number of turns: **Tailwind**, **Trick Room**, weather, terrain, and similar. If both Pokémon on a side use **Protect** on a turn when one of these effects is active, the opponent's effect burns a turn without producing any value. This is a meaningful way to reduce the impact of speed control and field conditions.

## Protect — Which Pokémon Run It

Most Pokémon in VGC carry **Protect**, but it is not universal. As a general starting point, aiming for roughly four of six Pokémon with a protecting move is a reasonable baseline — but this should be adjusted based on the team's actual needs rather than treated as a rule.

**Pokémon that commonly skip Protect:**

- **Extremely bulky Pokémon** (dedicated **Trick Room** setters, Pokémon with recovery moves): if a Pokémon takes well under 50% from most attacks and has ways to recover HP, **Protect** is less necessary. The staying power itself provides the same kind of security.
- **Choice item holders**: locked into one move, so **Protect** is not usable without switching.
- **Support Pokémon with full, active movepools**: a Pokémon running **Fake Out**, **Light Screen**, **Reflect**, and a utility move already has all four slots occupied with moves that contribute every turn. Adding **Protect** would require dropping something useful, and a Pokémon whose entire moveset is passive risks becoming an Island Pokémon. **Fake Out** and dual screens serve a pseudo-protective role already.
- **Highly offensive Pokémon on aggressive teams**: if a team's gameplan is to overwhelm through fast, consistent offense, additional coverage moves may produce more value than **Protect** — though this carries risk if the Pokémon cannot survive long enough to use that coverage.

**Pokémon that strongly benefit from Protect:**
Any Pokémon taking 50% or more from a significant portion of common threats has a strong reason to run **Protect**. The extra coverage or utility of a replacement move is only useful if the Pokémon is alive to use it — **Protect** often extends a Pokémon's lifespan long enough to justify the moveslot.

## Protect — Decision Framework for Individual Pokémon

When deciding whether a specific Pokémon should run **Protect**:

- **Estimate average damage taken**: if common threats deal 50% or more, **Protect** is likely necessary. If damage is consistently lower and recovery is available, **Protect** may be droppable.
- **Evaluate the alternative**: what move would replace **Protect**? If it is an offensive coverage move, assess how often the Pokémon will actually need it versus how much survivability is lost by not having **Protect**.
- **Consider the team's playstyle**: aggressive, offense-oriented teams may get less value from **Protect** than positioning-focused or switching-heavy teams. The value of **Protect** scales with how often the team needs to navigate between board states.

The clearest signal for whether a Pokémon needs **Protect** is empirical — running games without it and noticing how often its absence creates problems is a reliable way to calibrate.
