## Teambuilding — What It Is and Why It Matters

Teambuilding in competitive Pokémon is the thought process behind selecting which six Pokémon to use in a battle. Unlike casual play, competitive formats heal Pokémon between battles and prohibit in-battle items like Potions and Revives. The goal of competitive teambuilding is to construct a team of six Pokémon that maximizes the chances of winning battles. Teambuilding is considered an art rather than a strict science — personal preference, creativity, and adaptability all play a role — but results can be measured and refined through actual play.

## Teambuilding — Core Principles Overview

A strong competitive team is not simply the six Pokémon with the highest individual stats. Effective teambuilding requires evaluating several interrelated properties:

- **Synergy** — How well the Pokémon on a team work together. This includes complementary typings, move coverage, and role interactions. Synergy can take many forms beyond type matchups.
- **Consistency** — How reliably a team's options function across different matchups. A consistent team can accomplish its core gameplan against a wide range of opponents.
- **Pacing** — How quickly or slowly games tend to resolve with the team. Teams are often designed with a specific pace (aggressive, defensive, midrange) in mind.
- **Breadth** — How flexible the team is. Strong teams have backup plans when their primary win conditions are disrupted.
- **Depth** — How many layers of strategy the team contains. A deep team presents new options on repeated plays, making it harder for opponents to adapt.
- **Dynamicness** — In doubles, two Pokémon are controlled at once and must function together. Each Pokémon should work well alongside every other Pokémon on the team, not just its most common partner.
- **Relevance** — No team exists in isolation. A team's construction should account for the Pokémon and archetypes it is likely to face.

## Teambuilding — Evaluating and Iterating

Because teambuilding involves art and judgment, it is difficult to declare any single approach objectively correct. Player success at tournaments is significantly influenced by team choice, and even experienced builders fluctuate between strong finishes and underperformance as the format evolves. Winning with a team provides real feedback, but variance in results is expected. Iterating on a team after playing with it — observing which matchups feel weak, which Pokémon feel redundant, and which options are never used — is a core part of the teambuilding process.

## Teambuilding — Getting Started

New teambuilders do not need to build from scratch. Using teams constructed by experienced players is a valid and common approach for learning competitive formats. If building an original team, starting with a clear **win condition** and then selecting Pokémon that support or enable that condition is a practical first step. From there, evaluate the team against the checklist of synergy, consistency, pacing, breadth, depth, dynamicness, and relevance, and revise accordingly.
