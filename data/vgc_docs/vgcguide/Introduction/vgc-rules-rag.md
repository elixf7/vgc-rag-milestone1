## VGC Rules — Universal Battle Rules

Certain rules apply to every VGC format regardless of the current regulation or allowed Pokémon pool:

- **Last one standing**: The player with at least one Pokémon remaining when all opposing Pokémon are knocked out wins the game. Timer-based tiebreaks are a separate edge case.
- **Double battles**: Each player has up to two Pokémon active on the field simultaneously.
- **Bring six, pick four (Team Preview)**: Each player brings a roster of six Pokémon but selects only four to use in each game. Both full rosters are visible to both players during Team Preview before the four are chosen.
- **Species Clause**: Only one Pokémon of any given species (Pokédex number) may appear on a team. Two different forms sharing a Pokédex number (e.g. two regional variants of the same species) also cannot coexist on the same team.
- **Region-native / Battle-Ready requirement**: Pokémon must have been obtained in the current game or carry a battle-ready mark. This rule prevents use of move combinations that are no longer learnable in the current generation.
- **Mythical Pokémon banned**: Mythical Pokémon (e.g. **Mew**, **Celebi**, **Jirachi**) are never permitted in VGC formats.

## VGC Rules — Format Categories and Pokémon Pools

The Pokémon and mechanics permitted in VGC change periodically through different format regulations. Formats generally fall into one of three categories:

- **Regional Dex**: Only Pokémon native to the current game's regional Pokédex are allowed. This is typically the first format introduced for a new game generation. The restricted pool encourages teambuilding within a defined set of available options.
- **National Dex**: Pokémon from a broader cross-game Pokédex are permitted, typically excluding only the most powerful legendary Pokémon (called "restricted" Pokémon). This expanded pool significantly widens viable teambuilding options and typically produces more diverse metagames.
- **Restricted (informally "GS Cup")**: Normally banned restricted legendary Pokémon are permitted, usually with a limit of two per team. The remaining team slots come from the National Dex pool. Restricted formats require teams to be built around or against extremely high-powered Pokémon that fundamentally shape the metagame.

## VGC Rules — Restricted Pokémon and GS Cup

In Restricted formats, each player may use up to two restricted legendary Pokémon on their team of six. Restricted Pokémon are typically the most powerful legendaries — often referred to as "box legendaries" due to their prominence on game box art. The strategic implications of Restricted formats are significant:

- Teams are often built around a **restricted core** of two legendaries whose abilities, types, and move pools complement each other
- The remaining four team slots support the restricted pair with speed control, redirection, chip damage, or defensive utility
- Because both players may have restricted Pokémon, matchups frequently involve opposing restricted cores, and team selection during **Team Preview** becomes especially consequential
- Knowing common restricted pairings and their typical game plans is essential for building relevant teams in this format type

## VGC Rules — Format Rotation and Staying Current

VGC formats rotate periodically — historically every few months. Each new format may change the allowed Pokémon pool, introduce new mechanics, or shift which restricted Pokémon are permitted. Because the format changes frequently, strategic knowledge must be re-evaluated with each rotation: a team or archetype that was strong in one format may be significantly weaker or unplayable in the next. The official Play! Pokémon rules document is the authoritative source for current format end dates and allowed Pokémon.
