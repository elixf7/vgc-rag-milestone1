## VGC Match Structure — How a Battle Works

A VGC battle is a contest between two players, each directing their Pokémon to knock out the opponent's Pokémon. A Pokémon is knocked out when its health reaches zero; it is then unable to battle for the remainder of that game. The player who knocks out all of their opponent's active Pokémon first wins the game. Tournament matches are played as best-of-three: the first player to win two games wins the match.

## VGC Match Structure — Team Preview and Bring-Four

Before each game, both players view each other's full six-Pokémon rosters during **Team Preview**. Each player then privately selects four of their six Pokémon to bring into that game. The selected four are the only Pokémon available for that game; the remaining two cannot be used. This selection happens fresh before each game in a match, allowing players to adjust their four-Pokémon selection based on what they learned in prior games.

## VGC Match Structure — The Field and Switching

Each player has two Pokémon active on the field simultaneously — this is the defining doubles structure of VGC. The remaining two chosen Pokémon wait in the back and are not visible or targetable. At any point on their turn, a player may switch an active Pokémon out for one of their back Pokémon. Switching uses that Pokémon's action for the turn.

## VGC Mechanics — Turns and Turn Order

Each battle proceeds in turns. Both players simultaneously select actions for each of their two active Pokémon — neither player sees the other's choices before committing. Once both players have chosen, all four active Pokémon act in sequence. Turn order is determined by each Pokémon's Speed stat: higher Speed acts first. Some moves have increased or decreased priority, causing them to act before or after normal-speed moves regardless of Speed values. Managing turn order — through Speed stats, **Tailwind**, **Trick Room**, and **paralysis** — is a core strategic element of VGC.

## VGC Mechanics — Health and Damage

Every Pokémon has a health bar that represents its remaining durability. Moves that "deal damage" reduce the target's health. When health reaches zero, the Pokémon is knocked out and replaced by a back-line Pokémon (if one remains). Health bars are the primary real-time indicator of game state: a player with more remaining health across their Pokémon is generally in a stronger position.

## VGC Mechanics — Moves and Type Effectiveness

Each Pokémon can know up to four moves. Moves vary in function:

- **Damage moves**: Reduce the target's health. Damage output depends on the move's power, the attacking Pokémon's offensive stats, and the target's defensive stats.
- **Healing moves**: Restore a Pokémon's health rather than dealing damage.
- **Status and utility moves**: Do not directly change health but affect stats, turn order, weather, terrain, or other battle conditions.

**Type effectiveness** modifies how much damage a move deals based on the types of the move and the target:
- *Super effective*: The move deals bonus damage (the game displays "It's super effective!"). This occurs when a move's type is strong against the target's type.
- *Not very effective*: The move deals reduced damage. This occurs when the move's type is resisted by the target's type.
- Some type combinations result in immunity, causing no damage at all.

Type matchups are a foundational layer of both teambuilding (covering weaknesses, exploiting resistances) and in-battle decision-making (targeting the Pokémon most vulnerable to available moves).

## VGC Mechanics — Status Conditions and Luck

Pokémon battles include random elements that can swing a game's outcome. Common sources of variance include:

- **Critical hits**: Each move has a small chance to deal bonus damage regardless of the target's defensive stats. Cannot be fully controlled by either player.
- **Misses**: Some moves have an accuracy value below 100%, giving them a chance to fail entirely.
- **Freeze**: A status condition that prevents the afflicted Pokémon from acting. Duration is random and resolved turn-by-turn.
- **Paralysis**: A status condition with two effects — it reduces the afflicted Pokémon's Speed and gives each of its moves a chance to fail on any given turn.
- **Flinch**: Caused by certain moves; the afflicted Pokémon cannot act on the turn it flinches. Only relevant if the flinch-causing move acts before the target's move in turn order.

Managing and mitigating luck — through consistent positioning, redundant win conditions, and avoiding moves with low accuracy in high-stakes situations — is part of high-level VGC play.
