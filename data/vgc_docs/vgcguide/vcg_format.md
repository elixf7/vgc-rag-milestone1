## VGC Format — Core Rules and Structure

VGC (Video Game Championships) is the officially supported competitive format from The Pokémon Company. All VGC battles are **Double Battles**: two Pokémon fight against two opposing Pokémon simultaneously. This format rewards understanding how Pokémon work *alongside each other*, not just in isolation. The team-centric nature of doubles is the defining characteristic that separates VGC from single-battle formats.

## VGC Format — Bring-Six-Battle-With-Four

Each player constructs a team of six Pokémon but selects only four to bring into any given match, a decision made during **Team Preview**. Team Preview occurs before battle begins: both players see each other's full six-Pokémon roster, then independently choose which four to bring and in what lead order. The first two selected Pokémon are sent out immediately; the remaining two wait in the back. The player who knocks out all four of their opponent's chosen Pokémon wins.

This "bring four" structure is a major strategic layer. Teams must be built with flexible cores that can adapt to many opposing combinations, and match-to-match team selection requires reading what threats the opponent is likely to bring.

## VGC Fundamentals — Teambuilding Overview

Teambuilding in VGC involves selecting six Pokémon and customizing their moves, items, abilities, natures, and EV spreads. Every aspect of a Pokémon's build can be tuned, allowing players to express distinct styles and game plans. Key teambuilding considerations include:

- **Role coverage**: each Pokémon should contribute a defined role (setter, attacker, support, speed control, etc.)
- **Synergy**: Pokémon on the same team should complement each other's strengths and cover each other's weaknesses
- **Flexible cores**: because only four of six Pokémon are brought per match, teams benefit from multiple viable lead combinations and back-line options
- **Move selection**: moves are chosen not only for power but for the utility they provide within the team's game plan

Players may also use a team built by someone else rather than constructing their own from scratch.

## VGC Fundamentals — Battling Overview

In battle, the goal is to knock out all four of the opponent's chosen Pokémon before they do the same. With two Pokémon active at once, every turn involves choosing moves (or switching) for both active Pokémon simultaneously, making positioning and prediction central skills. Core battling concepts include:

- **Lead selection**: which two Pokémon to send out first, informed by Team Preview reads
- **Back-line management**: when to switch in reserves and which match-up the back Pokémon are held for
- **Targeting**: deciding which opposing Pokémon to attack each turn, including directing spread moves
- **Speed control**: managing **Trick Room**, **Tailwind**, **paralysis**, and other speed-altering mechanics to control turn order

## VGC Fundamentals — Double Battles vs. Singles

Because two Pokémon are active at once, VGC mechanics differ meaningfully from single-battle formats:

- **Spread moves** (e.g. **Earthquake**, **Surf**, **Discharge**) hit all adjacent Pokémon, including allies, adding both offensive and self-damage considerations
- **Abilities and moves that affect multiple targets** (e.g. **Intimidate**, **Fake Out**, **Follow Me**) are dramatically more impactful in doubles because they influence all active Pokémon
- **Support roles** (redirection, speed control, protection via **Protect**) are core to doubles team construction in a way they are not in singles
- Turn order is determined by Speed stat and priority, but with four active Pokémon each turn, managing who moves when is more complex

## VGC Fundamentals — Key Game Concepts

**Team Preview**: The phase before battle in which both players see all six of each other's Pokémon and choose their four. Strong team preview play involves identifying the opponent's likely core, anticipating which four they will bring, and selecting the four Pokémon and lead order best suited to counter them.

**Metagame (meta)**: The metagame refers to the set of Pokémon, strategies, and team archetypes that are commonly used at a given point in time. Understanding the meta helps players anticipate what they will face and build accordingly. The meta shifts as players discover new strategies and as the allowed Pokémon pool changes between regulations.

**Format / Regulation**: The specific ruleset defining which Pokémon are legal for a given competitive season. Formats are updated periodically by The Pokémon Company, altering the available Pokémon pool and therefore the strategic landscape.

## VGC Fundamentals — Format Accessibility

VGC does not require mechanical execution skills like reaction time or precise inputs. Strategic decisions — team selection, move choice, positioning — are the primary skill expression. This makes the format accessible regardless of age or gaming background, while still offering significant strategic depth for experienced players.

Players can participate through:
- **Ranked Doubles** ladder in the mainline Pokémon games
- **Pokémon Showdown** (a fan-made battle simulator where Pokémon can be manually configured without in-game catching or breeding)
- Community-run online tournaments
- The official in-person circuit, which culminates annually in the Pokémon World Championships
