# Switching, Momentum, and the Endgame

Switching is the quiet skill that separates strong players. A well-timed switch
absorbs a predicted attack with a resist, denies a super-effective hit, or resets
an Intimidate. The cost is a turn of initiative, so switch when the information or
the matchup gained outweighs the tempo lost.

Double-switching (moving both active Pokémon, or one active and bringing in from
the back) can completely reset a board, but it is risky against an opponent who
reads it and gets a free double-target. Switch into a Pokémon that walls the most
threatening move, not just the one that resists the attack you expect this turn.

Momentum in doubles means the opponent is reacting to you rather than the reverse.
Pivot moves (U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn, Parting Shot) preserve momentum by
dealing damage or debuffing while repositioning. Speed control (Tailwind, Trick
Room) and disruption (Fake Out, redirection) all serve to keep the opponent on the
back foot.

The endgame begins when each side is down to two or three Pokémon. Now the game
becomes a concrete calculation: enumerate the opponent's possible moves, check
which of your lines survives all of them, and find the sequence that guarantees a
win or avoids a loss. Speed tiers, remaining Protect, and any active speed-control
timers are decisive here. Count exactly: in a two-versus-two, knowing who moves
first and whether a key attack is a guaranteed KO often determines the winner
before a move is made.

Protect your win condition to the very end. It is usually correct to sacrifice a
spent Pokémon to keep your closer healthy, and to play around the single worst-case
opposing line rather than the most likely one when the game is on the line.
