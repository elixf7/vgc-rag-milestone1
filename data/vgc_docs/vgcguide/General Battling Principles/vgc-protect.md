## Protect — Role and Importance

**Protect** is one of the most important moves in competitive VGC. Because both players move simultaneously, Protect provides a unique form of information gathering and risk management unavailable from any other move — it guarantees a safe turn for one Pokémon while the game state develops around it. Understanding when to use it, when not to, and how to play around an opponent's use of it is a foundational skill.

## Protect — Reasons to Use It

Common situations where Protect is the correct or useful play:

- Preventing a knockout on a key Pokémon.
- Avoiding significant damage to keep a Pokémon healthy for later turns.
- Blocking **Fake Out** on turn one, preventing flinch and allowing a full turn of actions.
- Repositioning safely — giving a partner time to switch or set up without the Protecting Pokémon taking a hit.
- Stalling out the opponent's temporary field conditions: **Trick Room**, **Tailwind**, weather, terrain, or screens. Each turn of Protect burns one turn off the remaining duration.
- Saving one Pokémon while its partner deals with or switches into a current threat.

## Protect — Drawbacks and Opportunity Costs

Using Protect is not free. On the turn Protect is used, the opponent gains the ability to:

- Switch a Pokémon out for free.
- Set up speed control (**Trick Room**, **Tailwind**, etc.) without interference.
- Use a stat-boosting move.
- Attack into the partner Pokémon with confidence, doubling up damage onto it.
- Set up a **Substitute** or use a recovery move.

A key skill is distinguishing turns where Protect is necessary from turns where it is unnecessary and surrenders too much initiative. Protect that does not address the root problem — e.g. if the protected Pokémon will still be threatened on the following turn — provides no lasting benefit.

## Protect — Decision Framework

Questions to work through before using Protect on a given turn:

- **Is this Pokémon at risk of being knocked out or severely compromised this turn?** (e.g. burned, put to sleep, or otherwise made ineffective)
- **If protected, what does the next turn look like?** Is the threat resolved, or will Protect just delay the same problem by one turn?
- **What is the partner Pokémon doing this turn?** Can it address the current threat independently? If not, is a switch available?
- **Is there something on the opponent's side that needs to be stalled out?** And is stalling it out this turn strictly necessary, or can it wait?
- **Does the opponent have a way to benefit from the Protect?** Setup moves, free switches, and doubling into the partner are all things to account for.
- **Was Protect used on the previous turn?** Consecutive Protects succeed only one-third of the time. A failed consecutive Protect leaves the Pokémon completely unable to act for the turn.

## Protect — Consecutive Protects

The probability of a consecutive Protect succeeding is one-third, meaning it fails two-thirds of the time. A failed consecutive Protect wastes the turn entirely — the Pokémon takes full damage and contributes nothing. This makes consecutive Protects a high-risk play that should generally be treated as a last resort.

Situations where consecutive Protect may be justified despite the risk:

- Speed control (e.g. **Tailwind**, **Trick Room**) needs to be stalled out and no reliable switch options remain.
- A specific Pokémon must survive to win the game, and the only way to save it is to allow a partner to knock out the opposing threat.
- No better play exists for the Pokémon, and giving the opponent a free switch-in is unacceptable.

The general rule: attempt consecutive Protect only when all other options are worse, and losing without it is likely.

## Protect — Playing Around the Opponent's Protect

**Knowing which Pokémon carry Protect**: Usage data (injected by this system for the current format) indicates how frequently individual Pokémon run Protect. Pokémon with very low Protect usage can be attacked into more confidently, since the risk of wasting an attack to Protect is lower. Pokémon with very high Protect usage should generally be treated as having it until confirmed otherwise.

**Avoid hard predictions on Protect**: A common mistake is becoming so confident a Pokémon will Protect that it is ignored entirely, only for it to attack instead. Always account for the cost of being wrong when assuming a Pokémon will Protect.

**Forcing Protect**: Attacking into a Pokémon that is likely to Protect has value even if the attack does nothing. If the Pokémon does not Protect, significant damage or a knockout is achieved. If it does Protect, it has now used its Protect and cannot safely use it again on the following turn — the subsequent turn becomes significantly harder for that slot. Deliberately pressuring a Pokémon into Protecting can be a strategic tool for making future turns easier.
