## Preserving Low-HP Pokémon — Core Principle

A Pokémon's damage output, move access, and ability activation are entirely independent of its remaining HP. A Pokémon at 1 HP functions identically to one at full health in terms of what it can do on its turn. This means low-HP Pokémon retain potential value and should not be automatically sacrificed. The decision of whether to save or sacrifice a damaged Pokémon is a meaningful one that affects late-game positioning.

## Preserving Low-HP Pokémon — When It Is Worth Saving

Two conditions should both be satisfied before committing to switching out and preserving a low-HP Pokémon:

1. **A safe re-entry opportunity will exist later.**
2. **The Pokémon will be able to have a meaningful impact when it re-enters.**

If either condition is unlikely, it may be better to let the Pokémon be knocked out now rather than take risks with other team members to preserve it.

## Preserving Low-HP Pokémon — Safe Re-Entry Options

Ways to bring a preserved Pokémon back into the battle without it taking damage:

- **After a teammate is knocked out**: A free switch on the opponent's knockout turn.
- **Pivot moves**: Moves like **Volt Switch**, **U-Turn**, and **Parting Shot** used after the opponent has already attacked allow a clean switch without the incoming Pokémon taking a hit.
- **Switching into a resisted or immune attack**: If the incoming Pokémon is immune to the opponent's expected attack type (e.g. a Flying-type or Ghost-type switching into the relevant move), the switch is safe.
- **Switching while a partner secures a knockout**: If the partner knocks out the relevant threatening Pokémon on the same turn, the switch-in takes no damage.
- **Prediction-based switch**: Possible but risky — switching in anticipation that the opponent will make a specific move. The risk is that if the prediction is wrong, the switch-in may take significant damage.

## Preserving Low-HP Pokémon — Assessing Impact After Re-Entry

Once a safe re-entry path exists, assess whether the Pokémon can still contribute meaningfully:

**Speed and move priority**: Most Pokémon need to act before being knocked out to provide value. Whether a Pokémon can move first depends not just on its base Speed, but on the current speed context:
- If the opponent has speed control active (**Tailwind**, **Trick Room**), the low-HP Pokémon may be outsped now but valuable once the condition expires.
- If a single opposing Pokémon is responsible for outspeeding the low-HP Pokémon, removing that specific threat may restore the Pokémon's ability to act first.
- Priority moves (**Fake Out**, **Follow Me**, **Grassy Glide**, **Aqua Jet**, and similar) allow a Pokémon to act before faster opponents regardless of Speed investment.

**Entry abilities**: Some abilities activate on switch-in and provide value even if the Pokémon does not survive to attack. **Intimidate** is the most common example; weather-setting and terrain-setting abilities function similarly.

## Preserving Low-HP Pokémon — Sacrificial Value

Even if a Pokémon cannot attack effectively upon re-entry, it may still serve a sacrificial role:

- Triggering a teammate's on-entry ability by switching in and being knocked out.
- Providing a teammate a safe switch-in at the end of the turn.
- Allowing a teammate to reset stat drops or other negative effects on re-entry.
- Stalling out an opponent's field condition (**Trick Room**, **Tailwind**) by occupying space for additional turns.

## Preserving Low-HP Pokémon — Weighing Value Against Risk

The potential future value of a saved Pokémon must be weighed against the risk of the switch itself. Switches can go wrong — if the turn does not play out as expected, the switching process may leave the team in a worse position than if the low-HP Pokémon had simply been allowed to go down. The decision to save a Pokémon is correct when the expected future value of preserving it clearly outweighs the risks involved in doing so.
